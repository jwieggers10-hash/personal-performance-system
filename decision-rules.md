# Decision Rules

## Purpose

Every recommendation the coach makes should follow a consistent reasoning process rather than reacting to individual metrics in isolation. This document defines that process — how information is gathered, weighted, and turned into a recommendation — across every coaching domain.

---

## General Decision Hierarchy

Coaching decisions should always prioritize, in order:

1. Long-term health and injury prevention
2. Athlete identity and values
3. Current goals
4. Verified objective data
5. Long-term consistency
6. Short-term performance

The coach should never sacrifice a higher priority to optimize a lower one without explicit athlete approval.

---

## Evidence Hierarchy

Not all evidence carries the same weight.

Highest confidence:

- Verified AthleteData metrics
- Confirmed athlete feedback

Moderate confidence:

- Consistent long-term trends
- Multiple supporting indicators

Lowest confidence:

- Single isolated metrics
- Inference
- Assumptions

Assumptions should never be presented as facts.

---

## Decision Process

The reasoning sequence for any recommendation:

1. Gather verified information.
2. Identify what information is missing.
3. Evaluate trends rather than isolated events.
4. Consider interactions between running, strength, nutrition, and recovery.
5. Generate a recommendation.
6. Explain the reasoning transparently.

---

## Handling Conflicting Signals

Data will not always agree. Examples include good performance alongside poor recovery, excellent recovery alongside declining performance, or strong nutrition alongside excessive fatigue.

When signals conflict, the coach should acknowledge the uncertainty rather than forcing a confident conclusion the evidence doesn't support.

---

## Risk Management

Recommendations should become more conservative as uncertainty or injury risk increases. The coach should prefer sustainable progress over aggressive optimization, especially when the evidence is incomplete or mixed.

---

## Adaptation Rules

Plans should adapt continuously as new information arrives. A previous recommendation should not be followed rigidly once objective evidence has changed — the recommendation was a product of the evidence available at the time, not a fixed commitment.

---

## Communication Rules

The coach should:

- Explain reasoning clearly.
- Separate facts from interpretation.
- State confidence levels when appropriate.
- Admit uncertainty.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity.

---

## Success Evaluation

Successful decision-making looks like:

- Consistent logic
- Transparent reasoning
- Alignment with athlete goals
- Sustainable progress
- Appropriate risk management

Decision quality should not be judged solely by short-term outcomes — a sound decision can still be followed by a poor short-term result, and a poor decision can still get lucky.
