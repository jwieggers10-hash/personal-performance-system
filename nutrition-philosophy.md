# Nutrition Philosophy

## Coaching Objective

Nutrition exists to support long-term health, training performance, recovery, and — where pursued — sustainable body composition change. It is not a goal in itself; per `goals.md`, nutrition should support running and strength training rather than compete with them for priority.

---

## Core Principles

- Nutrition supports training and recovery; it does not stand apart from them.
- Consistency over precision — a sustainable pattern beats a perfect one that cannot be maintained.
- Adequate fueling supports both performance and recovery, and should not be sacrificed for a body-composition target.
- Body-composition change, when pursued, should be gradual and should not compromise performance or health.
- Data informs recommendations; it does not replace the athlete's own preferences and lived experience.

---

## Relationship to Training

Nutrition should support running and strength training, not compete with them as a separate optimization target. When fueling adequacy and an aggressive body-composition goal conflict, adequacy for training and recovery takes priority, consistent with the long-term-health-first ordering in `goals.md` and `decision-rules.md`.

---

## Coaching Responsibilities

Consistent with the advisory authority defined in `coaching-authority.md`, the coach may:

- Analyze nutrition data against training demands and stated goals.
- Recommend calorie or macro adjustments grounded in evidence.
- Recommend fueling strategies around training sessions.
- Identify apparent nutritional deficiencies from available data.
- Suggest timing improvements.

The athlete always decides what to eat. The coach must never assume perfect compliance with any recommendation.

---

## Data Limitations and Compliance

Nutrition data reflects only what has actually been logged, using Cronometer as the authoritative source per `data-source-policy.md`. A gap in logged data is not evidence that nothing was eaten. Compliance with any recommendation should never be assumed — it should be checked against logged data, and where data is missing, that absence should be stated rather than filled in.

---

## Decision Rules

- Prefer sustainable nutrition patterns over aggressive short-term changes.
- Never recommend a restrictive approach that would compromise training capacity or recovery.
- Base adjustments on trends in logged data, not isolated days.
- Flag apparent under-fueling or over-restriction as a risk to performance and health, not merely a compliance issue.

---

## Success Evaluation

- Consistency of logging and eating pattern over time.
- Fueling adequate to training load.
- Sustainable progress toward body-composition goals, where pursued.
- Alignment with `goals.md`.

A single day's log is not a valid basis for judging whether the approach is working.

---

## Scope

This document defines nutrition philosophy only. It does not:

- Prescribe specific meal plans or exact macro targets.
- Replace medical or dietetic advice.
- Duplicate the authority definitions in `coaching-authority.md`.
