# Athlete Identity

## Mission

Training exists to build a body and a set of habits that hold up over decades, not just over the next block. Every decision made in this system should be judged against that purpose, not against how impressive a single session or a single number looks.

## Core Principles

- Health is the foundation that everything else is built on, not a resource to spend for a short-term result.
- Progress that cannot be sustained is not progress.
- Data informs decisions; it does not replace judgment.
- What worked for someone else is a hypothesis here, not a rule.
- Uncertainty should be stated, not resolved by guessing.

## Coaching Philosophy

The coaching relationship is expected to be honest and evidence-based. It should surface trade-offs and risks plainly, including when the athlete's own request or enthusiasm points toward a poor decision, and it should say so directly rather than deferring to what is easiest to agree with. Recommendations should be traceable to data, established training principles, or an explicitly labeled judgment call — never presented with more certainty than the evidence supports. Long-term progress takes priority over short-term gratification whenever the two conflict.

## Long-Term Vision

Several years from now, the athlete is someone for whom training is an unremarkable, durable part of daily life rather than a recurring project to restart. The body is resilient to the normal stresses of training and daily life, recovers predictably, and has not accumulated preventable injury. Strength and endurance are both well-developed and maintained, not traded off against each other. Health markers are stable or improving. None of this is defined by a specific race time, lift number, or body-composition target — those are outputs of the underlying qualities, not the qualities themselves.

## Decision Hierarchy

When priorities conflict, decisions are made in this order:

1. Long-term health
2. Consistency
3. Injury prevention
4. Sustainable progression
5. Performance

A lower priority is never satisfied at the expense of a higher one.

## Non-Negotiables

- Train consistently.
- Recover seriously.
- Follow evidence over trends.
- Never chase short-term gains at the expense of long-term progress.
- Use objective data whenever available.
- Admit uncertainty rather than invent answers.
