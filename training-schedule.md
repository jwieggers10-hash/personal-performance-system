# Training Schedule

## Purpose

This document defines scheduling philosophy — how the coaching system should organize training across time — rather than a fixed training calendar. It does not define specific workouts or weekly templates; those are the product of applying this philosophy to the athlete's current state, not a permanent structure.

---

## Weekly Planning Philosophy

A schedule is a flexible working framework, revisited regularly, not a fixed calendar to be defended. It should be built around the athlete's actual recovery capacity and current goals (`goals.md`), balancing running and strength load rather than treating each in isolation.

---

## Running Scheduling Principles

Running is scheduled under the coach's full authority (`coaching-authority.md`), following the priorities set out in `running-philosophy.md`: aerobic development before intensity, gradual load increases, and adequate spacing around harder efforts. This document does not restate those principles — scheduling decisions should apply them, not redefine them.

---

## Strength Scheduling Principles

Strength sessions are placed around the athlete's existing progression system (`strength-philosophy.md`), which the coach does not redesign. The coach's scheduling role is to sequence and space strength sessions so they support running rather than compromise it, per the priority running holds when the two conflict.

---

## Recovery Integration

The schedule adapts to recovery signals (`recovery-policy.md`) rather than being fixed in advance. When available recovery data or trends indicate elevated risk, the schedule should flex — through reduced load, rescheduled sessions, or additional rest — rather than being defended as originally planned.

---

## Handling Missed Sessions

A missed session is not made up. Consistent with `running-philosophy.md` and `strength-philosophy.md`, the coach should resume from the athlete's current state rather than compressing training to catch up to where the original schedule assumed they would be.

---

## Handling Travel, Illness, and Unexpected Disruptions

Disruptions are expected, not exceptions to be fought. When illness, travel, or other unexpected circumstances arise, the schedule should be adapted conservatively — health and long-term consistency take priority over completing the original plan, consistent with the decision hierarchy in `decision-rules.md`.

---

## Periodic Review and Schedule Adjustments

The schedule should be reviewed periodically against `goals.md` and observed recovery and performance trends, and adjusted based on that evidence rather than followed rigidly. A schedule that no longer fits the athlete's current state should change, not be enforced for its own sake.
