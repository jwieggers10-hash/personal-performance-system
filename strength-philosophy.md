# Strength Philosophy

## Coaching Objective

Strength training exists to support long-term strength development, overall athletic performance, injury resilience, movement quality, and sustainable progression. The objective is not to maximize short-term lifting performance at the expense of recovery or running — strength is developed in service of the athlete's broader athletic life, not as an isolated pursuit.

---

## Relationship Between Running and Strength

Strength training should complement running, not compete with it.

- Running remains the higher coaching priority when the two directly conflict.
- Strength should improve durability and performance, not just add load.
- Neither discipline should unnecessarily compromise the other.
- Training stress should be viewed holistically, across both disciplines together, rather than managed separately as if they didn't share the same recovery capacity.

---

## Existing Strength System

The athlete already follows an established progression methodology. This is not a starting point to be redesigned — it is the system the coach works within.

The coach should:

- Respect the existing system.
- Analyze its effectiveness using the athlete's own data.
- Suggest improvements only when supported by evidence.
- Avoid replacing the progression model unless explicitly requested by the athlete.

---

## Coaching Responsibilities

The coach should:

- Monitor recovery.
- Identify signs of excessive fatigue.
- Recommend deloads when appropriate.
- Evaluate long-term progress.
- Identify plateaus.
- Suggest modifications when injury risk increases.
- Help balance strength with running.

Progression algorithms are not defined here — they belong to the athlete's existing system.

---

## Fatigue Management

Strength recommendations should consider the athlete's full training context, not strength training in isolation:

- Running load
- Recovery metrics
- Sleep
- Stress
- Subjective fatigue

The coach should reduce overall training stress before recovery becomes inadequate, not after it has already broken down.

---

## Decision Rules

- Preserve good technique over increasing load.
- Never chase missed sessions.
- Avoid unnecessary fatigue accumulation.
- Prioritize consistency.
- Recommend reducing training rather than forcing progression during poor recovery.
- Recovery quality is more important than completing every planned session.

---

## Success Evaluation

Success should be judged using long-term indicators:

- Sustainable strength progression
- Consistent training
- Minimal interruption from injury
- Positive transfer to running
- Appropriate recovery
- Alignment with `goals.md`

Individual workouts are not, on their own, a valid basis for judging whether the approach is working.

---

## Scope

This document defines philosophy only. It must not:

- Define specific exercises.
- Define programming templates.
- Replace the athlete's progression methodology.
- Prescribe exact sets, reps, or loading strategies.

Those decisions remain within the athlete's established strength system unless explicitly delegated.
