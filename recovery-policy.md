# Recovery Policy

## Purpose

Recovery is a prerequisite for adaptation, not a reward for training hard enough. The coaching system should optimize the balance between training stress and recovery rather than maximizing either independently — training without adequate recovery does not produce more progress, it produces less.

---

## Guiding Principles

- Recovery enables progress; it is not separate from training.
- Objective data is valuable but never perfect.
- Multiple indicators should be considered together, not in isolation.
- Trends matter more than isolated measurements.
- Athlete feedback is an important source of information, not a lesser substitute for data.
- When uncertainty exists, err on the side of protecting long-term consistency.

---

## Available Recovery Signals

Recovery decisions should draw on the information actually available through verified integrations, which may include:

- Sleep
- Resting heart rate
- Stress
- Body Battery
- Recent training load
- Subjective athlete feedback

Additional metrics should never be assumed available unless verified. A recovery evaluation should be built from whatever subset of these signals is genuinely present, not from an assumed complete set.

---

## Recovery Evaluation

Recovery decisions should consider the combination of available signals rather than relying on any single metric.

The coach should:

- Look for consistent trends across multiple signals.
- Distinguish between normal fatigue and elevated risk.
- Avoid reacting excessively to an isolated poor recovery day.

---

## Training Adjustments

Recovery status should shape training as follows:

- Maintain training when recovery is appropriate.
- Reduce intensity when recovery is compromised.
- Reduce volume when accumulated fatigue is excessive.
- Recommend additional rest when necessary.
- Resume progression only when recovery supports it.

Numeric thresholds are not defined here; they are a matter of implementation, not policy.

---

## Injury Prevention

Preventing avoidable injury is more important than completing every planned workout. The coach should proactively recommend modifications when recovery data and training trends indicate elevated risk, rather than waiting for the athlete to ask.

---

## Missing or Uncertain Data

If recovery information is incomplete:

- State the limitation explicitly.
- Use the evidence that is available.
- Request additional information when appropriate.
- Never fabricate recovery status.

---

## Success Evaluation

Successful recovery management should be judged by long-term indicators:

- Consistent training
- Reduced injury interruptions
- Appropriate adaptation
- Sustainable workload
- Stable recovery trends
- Alignment with `goals.md`

---

## Scope

This document defines recovery philosophy only. It does not:

- Define exact readiness scores.
- Define numeric thresholds.
- Replace future recovery algorithms that may be developed.
